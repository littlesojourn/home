关于此行

A journey to where tea and clay begin

Every cup of tea tells two stories: where it was grown, and what it was made in. This journey follows both to their source.

We travel first to Wuyishan (武夷山) in Fujian, the cradle of rock oolong, where tea grows in the crevices of Danxia (丹霞) cliffs and the culture of tea has accumulated over a thousand years. Then we move to Jingdezhen (景德镇) in Jiangxi, the porcelain capital of the world for seventeen centuries, where kilns still fire and artists still make.

"The pot remembers the hands that made it. The tea remembers the mountain."

We move between cities by high-speed rail, a four-hour arc from one province to the next, from mountain to plain, from leaf to clay. The shift in landscape, light, and material culture is palpable.

武夷山 · Fujian (福建)

Wuyishan

A UNESCO World Cultural and Natural Heritage Site. The Danxia (丹霞) landscape, with red sandstone cliffs carved by the Nine Bend River (九曲溪), is the same geology that gives Wuyi rock oolong its mineral character, 岩韵 (yán yùn). The mountains also carry a thousand years of influences from the three teachings, Confucianism, Buddhism, and Taoism, a coexistence that gave Wuyishan much of its singular depth.

景德镇 · Jiangxi (江西)

Jingdezhen

China's porcelain capital for seventeen centuries. Jingdezhen's kilns supplied the imperial court from the Song (宋) dynasty onward, and its output shaped ceramic traditions from Korea to Persia to Delft. The geological reason: fine deposits of kaolin and petuntse beneath the city, in combination, found almost nowhere else.

The Nine Bend River & Mother Trees

AM Nine Bend River bamboo raft — 九曲溪竹筏
Early departure from Xingcun (星村) pier. Two hours drifting the nine bends downstream — past 3,000-year-old hanging coffins, Song dynasty cliff inscriptions, and Jade Maiden Peak (玉女峰) reflected in still water. Each bend marked in red calligraphy on the rock face, counting down: 九 to 一
PM Jiulong Ke mother tree Da Hong Pao — 九龍窠母樹大紅袍
The original six Da Hong Pao bushes — the most famous tea plants on earth, now no longer harvested but still growing on the cliffside at Jiulong Ke (九龍窠)

Tongmu Pass — Where Black Tea Began

All day Excursion to Tongmu Pass — 考察桐木關
Deep into the Wuyishan Nature Reserve, about 1.5–2 hours from the city. Tongmu Pass is where Lapsang Souchong (正山小種) was created — the world's first black tea, discovered here in the 17th century when passing soldiers forced the drying of tea leaves over pine fires, accidentally producing the smoke-cured style that would eventually become the model for all black tea globally.

Arrival & Imperial Kiln Site

09:00Train departs Wuyishan. Arrive Jingdezhen ~10:00. Check in
PM Taoyang Li Imperial Kiln Museum and site  — 陶陽里御窯博物館
Built over actual Ming and Qing dynasty (明清) imperial kiln ruins. Understand the production system of the imperial kilns and the history of royal-use porcelain. View precious excavated ceramics and key archaeological finds

Nanlu Ruins — 南麓遺址
The imperial kiln factory excavation site — key archaeological discoveries spanning Jingdezhen's thousand-year manufacturing history

Longzhu Pavilion — 龍珠閣
Overlook the old city of Jingdezhen and the imperial kiln district from above

Xu Family Wood-fired Kiln — 徐家柴窯
A kiln built in the late Ming dynasty (明末), operating for over 300 years. Rebuilt and re-fired in 2016 after nearly 40 years dormant. Understand traditional wood-firing technique and kiln structure; learn how pine-fired heat shapes porcelain texture and glaze colour in ways no electric or gas kiln can replicate.

Ceramics Museum & Mr. Zhang's Workshop

AM Jingdezhen Ceramics Museum — 景德鎮陶瓷博物館
Systematically trace Jingdezhen's thousand-year manufacturing history. Appreciate masterworks and the evolution of technique across dynasties, Song celadon, Yuan blue-and-white, Ming imperial ware, Qing famille rose. Build a complete historical foundation before entering working studios
PM Mr. Zhang's Workshop — 張先生工作坊 · Xindu Ceramics Garden (新都陶瓷園)
A small-scale handmade ceramics workshop operating at the intersection of craft and commerce. Follow the production flow from form to firing, and explore how contemporary functional ware navigates the balance between artistic intention and market reality. We'll time our visit to coincide with a gas kiln unloading, if the schedule aligns.

出发前准备

Things to know before

!
Install WeChat — do this now

WeChat is how we communicate as a group, how you pay for almost everything in China, and how you access DiDi (rides) and Mei Tuan (food delivery and local services). Set it up before you leave and link your credit card to WeChat Pay before the trip. Also link a card to Alipay as a backup — both WeChat Pay and Alipay now accept international Visa and Mastercard.

Travel documents

  • Passport — valid at least 6 months beyond November 1, 2026
  • Chinese visa — many nationalities now qualify for visa-free entry. Confirm your eligibility. This policy changes frequently — verify with a recent source, not information older than a few months
  • Travel insurance — required. Should cover medical, cancellation, and lost luggage
  • Printed copies — carry printed copies of passport, visa, and insurance. Accommodation registration requires your passport at every check-in

Apps to install

  • WeChat — communication, payments, QR codes, DiDi, Mei Tuan. The most essential app for China
  • Alipay — secondary payments. Link an international card before you arrive
  • VPN — install and test before landing. Cannot be downloaded once inside China. Google, Instagram, and most Western apps are blocked without one
  • Amap (高德地图) — China's standard mapping app. Google Maps does not work reliably
Set up all apps and test your VPN before you land. Once in China, you are behind the Great Firewall and cannot download blocked services.

Data & SIM

  • eSIM (recommended) — Trip.com sells China-compatible eSIMs that are easy to install before departure. Arrive already connected. Works with most modern unlocked phones
  • Physical SIM at the airport — available from China Mobile and China Unicom on arrival, requires passport. Note: by the time you buy it at the airport, you are already behind the Firewall — install any VPNs and apps before landing
  • International roaming — works, but data costs can be high. Check your carrier's China plan before departure
The group WeChat runs all logistics throughout the trip. A working data connection from Day 1 is important.

Money

  • WeChat Pay and Alipay — nearly all transactions in China: street food, markets, temples, taxis. Link an international Visa or Mastercard before you arrive
  • Cash RMB (¥) — carry some. ¥500–1,000 in small bills covers older establishments and rural spots that don't take mobile payment
  • ATMs — Bank of China and ICBC are most reliable for international cards. Notify your bank before departure to avoid security blocks

Weather & packing

  • Wuyishan — 13–22°C (55–72°F). Cooler mornings and evenings, perfect for hiking through the tea mountains. October is one of the drier, more comfortable months
  • Jingdezhen — 10–25°C (50–77°F), dropping noticeably toward end of October
  • Layers — the gap between morning and afternoon is real. Light down or fleece, breathable mid-layers
  • Windbreaker — useful on the river raft and mountain trails
  • Comfortable walking shoes — cobblestones, trails, studio paths. Comfort over style

Health & practical

  • Medications — bring a sufficient supply. Include a note or prescription if possible
  • Physical level — easy to moderate. No vigorous hiking.
  • Electricity — 220V, Type A/C/I outlets. Most devices charge via USB. Universal adapter recommended
